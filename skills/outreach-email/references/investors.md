# Investors

Founder emails to VCs and angels: cold pitches, forwardable intro blurbs, follow-ups after a meeting, momentum updates during a raise, replies to a pass, and monthly updates to prospects. Everything in SKILL.md applies, with one shift: for investors, **traction and fit are the hook**. A personal bridge can help, but the email must be strong enough to earn a meeting on its facts alone.

Register: the user's voice, direct and numbers-forward. No hype, no jargon, no origin story.

---

## What to get from the user

From `background.md` (Venture section) or the conversation, and **re-confirm the numbers before drafting**, because they go stale fast and inflated numbers end relationships:

- What the company does, in one plain sentence a non-expert gets.
- Stage and the raise: amount, instrument, lead or what's committed so far.
- Traction with dates: revenue or ARR, growth rate, users, named customers, pilots, LOIs, each labeled for what it is.
- Team: the one fact that makes this team the right one.
- Why now.
- Warm path to this investor, if any.
- Deck link (a view-tracked link, not an attachment).

## Research the investor (fit before charm)

Follow `finding-the-connection.md`, focusing on: their fund's thesis, stage and check size (if they don't do this stage, say so to the user and stop), portfolio companies adjacent to the user's (and any that compete, which rules them out), partner posts or tweets about this market, recent deals. Find the specific **partner**, not the fund's generic inbox.

The investor hook is usually "you invested in / wrote about X, and we're [the specific version of that bet]." A shared hobby can be a second line, never the lead.

## The cold email

About three sentences, readable in under a minute on a phone:

1. **What the company does**, plainly. No "revolutionizing," no "AI-powered platform for."
2. **Why they should care**: the two strongest facts (a metric with a date, a named customer, a notable co-investor, a team fact), as two short bullets if there are numbers. Plus one line on why *this* investor.
3. **The ask**: what the user wants and the easy next step. Default: "We're raising $[X] [stage]. Happy to send the deck; would a 20-minute call in the next two weeks make sense?" Sources disagree on meeting ask vs. "want the deck?"; pick the meeting ask when traction is strong and the fit is exact, the deck offer when either is weaker, and say which you chose.

Sign with name, title, and company URL. Send from a company address.

**Subject line: the strongest fact, not a teaser.** Investors triage on it: `[Company]: [what it is in a few words], [one metric], raising [stage]`, e.g. `Tallyworks: maintenance ops software, $18K MRR, raising seed`. Lowercase-hook subjects from SKILL.md work for a door-opener to an investor (see below), not a pitch.

**Length:** short. Investors spend under three minutes on a whole deck; the email gets less. Anything beyond what-it-is, two proof points, and the ask reads like spam to a busy partner.

**Deck:** link, don't attach, in the first email, or offer it. Never ask for an NDA.

**Warm vs cold.** A warm intro from someone the investor trusts (ideally a founder they backed) is best, but a strong cold email beats a weak warm intro. Many seed investors fund cold emails; volume matters too (founders typically contact dozens of investors per round), so each email should be quick to adapt, not a from-scratch essay.

### Door-opener to an investor

When the user isn't raising yet: no ask for money. Engage with something the partner wrote or a bet they made, share one real data point or observation from building in that space, and ask their view. Then add them to updates (below) when they reply. "Build the relationship before you need it" is how many rounds actually start.

## Forwardable intro blurb

When someone offers to introduce: ask them to check with the investor first (double opt-in; good investors expect it). Then write a short email addressed *to the investor*, so the connector just forwards it: what the company does, the two strongest facts, why this investor, the ask, deck link. After the intro, move the connector to BCC in the reply and thank them.

## After a pitch meeting

- **Same day:** thanks in a line, answers to the questions they raised (with the data), whatever they asked for, and the next step as the user understood it. "You asked about retention: here's the cohort chart. Month-3 retention is 71%."
- **Ask what they need to decide, and by when.** Treat anything short of a definite offer as not yet.
- **Momentum updates every few days to two weeks**, only with real news: commitments ("$700K of $1M committed"), a new customer, a lead. A reasonable close date is fine to mention once it's real.
- **Data room** only after real interest. Early on, the deck and a short summary are enough.

## Replying to a pass or "not now"

Short, gracious, no arguing. Thank them; optionally ask for one honest piece of feedback (easy to skip); ask if they'd like monthly updates. Don't ask an investor who passed for intros to other investors; it can work against the user.

> Thanks for the quick, clear answer, [Name], and for the time. If there's one honest piece of feedback you'd share, I'd value it, no pressure. Mind if I add you to our monthly update? I'd love to reconnect once we hit [specific milestone].

## Investor updates (to prospects and existing investors)

Subject: `[Company] [Month Year] update`. Monthly, short, same format every time:

1. **Key numbers** (revenue or users, growth, cash and runway for existing investors). Keep the same metrics each month, including the one that went down.
2. **Highlights**, three at most.
3. **Lowlights or challenges**, honestly.
4. **Asks**: specific ("intros to heads of ops at 200+ unit property managers").

BCC the list. Bullets and bold labels are fine; it's a report.

## Honesty rules for traction

- Label everything accurately: signed customer vs. pilot vs. LOI; paying vs. free users; ARR vs. one-time revenue.
- Growth percentages from a tiny base get the absolute numbers next to them ("2 to 20 customers").
- No projections dressed as results; no top-down "1% of a $50B market" TAM.
- Pre-revenue: show speed and team instead ("built in six weeks, 3 pilots live") rather than a weak traction line.
- If a number in `background.md` is more than a month or two old, ask before using it.

## Never

- "Revolutionizing," "disrupting," "the Uber of," "billion-dollar opportunity"
- NDAs, "Dear Sir/Madam," mass BCC on a pitch, identical blasts with the fund name swapped
- A fake "RE:" subject to fake a thread
- "Would love to pick your brain" / "grab coffee to compare notes" with no substance
- Pitching over LinkedIn or X DMs when an email address is findable
- Flattery in place of fit ("huge fan of your fund")
